# Sending authority — what Claude may send without asking

**Granted by Andrew, 2026-11-17**, for the account
`andrew@pacificresearchllc.com`. This file is the standing record. A
scheduled run that needs to know whether it may send reads this, not a
conversation.

## May be sent without asking

- Responses to **sources sought** notices and **RFIs** (nonbinding market
  research).
- **Clarification questions** to a contracting officer, where the notice
  permits questions and by the channel it names.
- **Supplier and teaming inquiries**, and **requests for quotes** to
  suppliers.
- One **follow-up** per unanswered message, 5 to 7 business days later.

## Never sent without Andrew

- A **quote**, a **price**, or a rough-order-of-magnitude figure to the
  Government. A quote is a binding offer.
- A **proposal** or any submission to a solicitation.
- **Certifications or representations** made on PRG's behalf.
- **Agreements** of any kind: NDAs, teaming agreements, exclusivity,
  subcontracts, purchase orders.
- Anything that **names a partner** before a signed agreement exists.
- Anything that **argues** with a contracting officer's decision
  (CLAUDE.md standing order).

## Checks before every send

1. **Read the notice's own instructions.** If it requires a portal, a
   form, a file format, a subject line, or prohibits email, do that
   instead. If it requires an **attachment**, stop: the Outlook connector
   cannot attach files, so prepare the draft and the file and hand both
   to Andrew.
2. **Search Sent Items by notice number first.** A hit means skip. Never
   a second copy, a corrected copy, or an unprompted follow-up.
3. **Every factual claim traces to** `capture/library/company_profile.md`
   or the notice. Individual experience of the Managing Director is
   labelled as individual, never as PRG contract performance.
4. **Honor do-not-contact.** Check the supplier and contact records
   before sending; an opt-out is permanent.
5. **Record it** in the contracting database the same run: the message,
   the stage change, the follow-up date.

No message is sent to meet a count. If nothing qualifies, nothing goes.

## Never reference what is not there (Andrew, 2026-11-20)

No email, draft, or report to Andrew may say a thing was sent,
attached, enclosed, linked, or scheduled unless the thing itself is
actually in it. This is absolute, in every lane.

- **Meeting invites (Zoom, Teams, any platform).** Never write "I've
  sent an invite", "invite to follow", "join link below", or "a Teams
  meeting is scheduled" unless a real join URL is in that same message
  body, or a real calendar event exists with the join link on it. Read
  it back from the created event before saying so. If no tool can
  produce a real link, say so to Andrew plainly and propose times in
  text instead; never imply a link exists.
- **Attachments.** Never write "attached is", "please find attached",
  or "enclosed" in a draft unless the file is on that draft. The
  Outlook connector cannot attach, so a draft that needs a file is
  written without that wording, and Andrew is told **at draft time**,
  once, with the draft link, the file's full path, and the deadline.
  A missing attachment discovered at the deadline is this rule failing
  (75N94027R00005 RFI, 2026-11-19).
- **Links.** Every "see link", "available here", or URL reference
  carries the actual URL, checked to resolve.
- **Reporting to Andrew.** Never tell Andrew something was sent,
  scheduled, or attached without the evidence: the Sent Items hit, the
  event, or the file on the message. If it was not verified, say so.

## Partner outreach (Andrew, 2026-11-20; replaces the 11/17 volume target)

- **One potential partner per contract.** Write to one firm about a given
  notice. Do not write to a second firm while the first is queued,
  contacted, interested or teaming. If the first declines, or sends nothing
  back within **72 hours** (Andrew, 11/20), it is marked NO REPLY and the
  next firm may be asked. (On 11/17-18, seven firms were
  written to about the same VA Orlando notice; that is what this rule ends.)
- **General introductions build the contact list.** When no open contract
  fits a firm, send a contract-free introduction instead: who PRG is, the
  lanes, and a request to stay in touch. A handful a day, not volume for its
  own sake.
- **When a partner is chosen,** every other firm that showed interest in
  that contract gets a short, courteous close-out: PRG has teamed for that
  one, and would like to stay in touch for future work.
- **The bar does not move.** Every message names a specific notice or a
  specific reason for that firm, uses facts from the company profile, and
  tells the recipient they can decline further contact.
- **Who is worth writing to.** On an SDVOSB set-aside the partner must be a
  certified SDVOSB (similarly situated) or the work must be done by PRG's own
  W2 hires; on a total small business set-aside any small business
  qualifies. A requirement one specialist firm would perform end to end is a
  pass for PRG as prime (CLAUDE.md kill 6): write to that firm about teaming
  with PRG as a member, not about subcontracting to PRG.
- **Send window:** 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM in the recipient's time zone where
  practical.

## Meeting lead time (Andrew, 11/20)

No tight turnarounds, ever. Any call or meeting PRG proposes is at least
**one week out** from the day it is proposed, so there is time to meet the
people before anything is applied for or committed. Check Andrew's
calendar first, offer two or three slots inside the other party's stated
availability, and never propose same-day or next-day times. If the other
party asks for something sooner, offer the earliest slot a week out and
let them come back. A meeting invite is not sent until the time is agreed,
and an online-meeting link is checked to exist before it is relied on
(on 11/19 the Teams link was never created and the call fell back to phone).
