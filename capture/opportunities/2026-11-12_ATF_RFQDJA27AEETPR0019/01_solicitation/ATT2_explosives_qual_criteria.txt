                                    ATTACHMENT 2
  CLIN 0001: Explosives Specialist - Law Enforcement/Regulatory Enforcement Instructor

                                Contractor Qualification Criteria


Technical Proficiency:

1. Instructors must possess law enforcement explosives/explosion investigation, explosives
   regulatory enforcement, explosives training skills, or have unique experience, education, and
   training to support ATF’s explosives enforcement and training missions. These trainings and
   experiences are highly regarded skill sets and are required for specialty programs at NCETR.

2. Areas of expertise desired for Explosives/Regulatory Enforcement Instructors includes, but is
   not limited to:

   •   Criminalistics and Forensic Science         •   Explosives Risk Mitigation
   •   Destructive Device Classification and       •   Explosives Testing Procedures
       Reconstruction                              •   Explosives Theft Investigation
   •   Electricity and Electronics                 •   Explosion Investigation and Analysis
   •   Explosives Identification, Applications,    •   Federal Explosives Laws and Regulations
       and Effects                                 •   Fire and Explosion Dynamics
   •   Explosives Classification                   •   Fire Investigation and Arson Enforcement
   •   Explosives Disposal Techniques                  Techniques
   •   Explosives Laws and Regulations             •   Homemade Explosives Identification,
   •   Explosives Manufacturing                        Process, and Disposal
   •   Explosives Range Procedures

3. ATF uses a variety of instructional methods, including classroom lectures and
   demonstrations, practical exercises (PE), and live range operations. The contractor shall
   provide input for the revision or amendment of the lectures, demonstrations, and exercises to
   provide updated materials, as appropriate and necessary for the course requirements.

4. Many courses require the instructor to provide both classroom as well as range instruction.

5. Contractor rates will be used for all services including, but not limited to, instruction, range
   activities, and course development.

6. Exceptions may be made to contractor instructors who possess unique qualifications based on
   commercial explosives industry experience, research and development experience, or
   graduate level education in explosives, engineering, and scientific related disciplines.

Education/Experience and Certification Requirements:

7. Bachelor’s degree from an accredited college or university with a minimum of ten years of
   subject matter related work experience (i.e., law enforcement and military, with a major
   portion of this experience in the explosives and investigative operations arena) -Preferred.

   A high school education with a diploma or a GED equivalency certificate and 15 years of

   A Graduate degree, with a minimum of 5 years working, instructing, or conducting research
   in the subject area at a level exceeding the students to be taught.


8. Special consideration may be given to:
       •      Those who possess experience instructing for ATF/NCETR explosives training
              programs within three years of proposal submission
       •      ATF Certified Explosives Specialists
       •      ATF Certified Fire Investigators

9. Explosives range instructors must have completed the ATF Advanced Explosives Disposal
   Techniques (AEDT) school or have documented experience with providing range instruction
   for ATF explosives training held at the National Center for Explosives Training and
   Research (NCETR).

10. Instructors teaching exploitation or disassembly must have completed the ATF Explosives
    Evidence Exploitation (E3) school and be graduates of either Hazardous Devices School –
    Basic Course or Naval School Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD).

11. Successful completion of an instructor training course or demonstrated experience providing
    instruction in either a professional or academic classroom setting.

12. Provide approach to maintain the currency of subject matter expertise and updates to
    continuing education and required certifications.

13. Provide verifying documentation, upon request, (e.g., certificate of successful completion or
    attendance) demonstrating maintenance of subject matter currency through recent continuing
    education and training activities in the subject matter being taught. Subject matter currency
    may also be validated through the publication of subject matter related articles and research
    in nationally recognized professional journals or trade magazines.
