**What PRG does not have, and must never imply it has:** no laboratory,
no wet-bench capability, no animal facility, no GMP capacity, no
clinical site, no facility clearance, and no existing IP.

That list is not a weakness to write around. It is the input to Gate 1,
and it is why the default mechanism is STTR: under STTR the partnering
research institution holds the lab, the site, and often the IP, and PRG
holds the award.

**What SDVOSB is worth here:** almost nothing, and the documents should
say so by omission. NIH SBIR/STTR has no veteran set-aside and no
veteran evaluation preference. It is one line in a company background,
the same way it is in the international lane. Any application that leans
on it is misreading the program.

## Operator — Andrew O'Donnell, Managing Director

### The hard constraint that drives the whole system

Andrew is **concurrently job searching for a full-time role.**

- **SBIR** requires the PD/PI to be **primarily employed** by the small
  business at the time of award and throughout the project. A full-time
  role elsewhere breaks that. PRG therefore cannot satisfy the SBIR PI
  rule and must not pretend otherwise.
- **STTR** allows the PD/PI to be employed by the partnering non-profit
  research institution, provided they hold a formal appointment with or
  commitment to PRG — which need not involve salary — and commit at
  least 10% effort.

So: **default to STTR.** Surface SBIR only where the work is something
Andrew could credibly PI himself as a solo operator, and surface it with
the employment conflict stated in the row rather than buried.

### The asset most applicants do not have

Two things, and every strong application should be built on them:

1. **Real relationships at Stanford Department of Medicine** — an actual
   path to a PD/PI and a partner letter, not a cold email.
2. **Hands-on knowledge of how clinical trial operations fail at the
   site level** — what actually goes wrong in startup, activation,
   accrual, retention, deviation, and data quality, from having run it.

Most STTR applicants have a technology and go looking for a clinical
problem. PRG has the inverse, and that is the position to write from.

### How that maps to fit scoring

The pipeline scores three tiers. The tiering is the honest part — it is
what stops "adjacent to healthcare" from scoring as capability.

**Tier A — the thing PRG has that others do not.** Site-level trial
operations: study startup and site activation, protocol deviations and
feasibility, accrual, recruitment and retention, source data
verification, risk-based monitoring, query resolution, clinical data
management, the regulatory binder, 21 CFR Part 11, GCP, CTMS, eConsent,
decentralized trials, and REDCap / OnCore / Medidata as the system of
record.

**Tier B — real adjacent capability.** Clinical engineering and HTM,
device integration and interoperability, EHR and clinical workflow,
health informatics, CDISC / FHIR / OMOP, real-world data and registries,
digital endpoints, remote patient monitoring, human factors.

**Tier C — credible but thin.** Implementation and dissemination
science, health disparities, community engagement, workforce training,
global health, Portuguese/Spanish-language work, veteran and military
health, emergency medical and point-of-care.

**Negative — pulls the score down.** Medicinal chemistry, small
molecules and drug candidates, gene editing, protein engineering,
monoclonal antibodies, nanoparticles, organoids and cell lines, assay
and biomarker development, microfluidics, formulation, PK. Not because
the science is uninteresting, but because PRG cannot supply the
STTR-mandatory 40% of the research effort on any of it.

## Standing honesty rules for this lane

Borrowed from `intl/GATE.md`, and they apply just as hard here:

- Never claim a laboratory, a clinical site, a patient population, an
  existing dataset, or IP that PRG does not have.
- Never name a Stanford investigator, department, or center as a partner
  in any document before that person has agreed in writing. The partner
  map above says who PRG would *ask*, not who has said yes.
- Never describe Andrew as the PD/PI of an application whose PI must be
  primarily employed by PRG while the job search is live.
- Prior-award counts, budget caps, and deadlines go in a document only
  with the source they were read from.
