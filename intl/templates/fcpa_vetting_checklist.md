## 1. Identity and legitimacy
- [ ] Legal name and registration number verified against the country's
      company registry (name the registry and the date checked)
- [ ] Verifiable physical business address (not a mail drop)
- [ ] Years in operation, sector, and approximate size
- [ ] Website / public presence reviewed
- [ ] Named owners, principals, and beneficial owners recorded below

## 2. Sanctions and exclusions screen (Rail 1)
- [ ] OFAC SDN / consolidated search — entity **and every principal**
      (https://sanctionssearch.ofac.treas.gov/) — result + date saved
- [ ] BIS Entity List / Denied Persons
- [ ] SAM.gov exclusions (if any U.S. nexus)
- [ ] UN consolidated list (https://scsanctions.un.org/consolidated/)
- [ ] Adverse-media search on the entity and principals, in the local
      language as well as English

## 3. Government-official proximity — ask directly, record the answer
- [ ] Is any owner, principal, employee, or **immediate family member**
      of any of them a government official, political-party official,
      candidate, or employee of a state-owned enterprise?
      Answer: ______________________
- [ ] If YES → **escalate to counsel before proceeding.** This is not
      automatically disqualifying, but it is never handled informally.
- [ ] Does the party hold any current government contract or license
      that depends on the officials involved in this procurement?

## 4. Relationship to the buyer
- [ ] Any current or former relationship with the buying entity, its
      staff, or the evaluators?
- [ ] Was the party **recommended by anyone at the buyer**? Record who.
      A buyer-recommended intermediary is a red flag, not a shortcut.

## 5. Compensation structure
- [ ] Compensation is for **defined work**, at a rate justified by that
      work — not a success fee, commission, or percentage of award
- [ ] Payment is by **bank transfer to the contracted entity's own
      account, in the contract's jurisdiction** — bank details recorded
- [ ] **No cash. No third-party payees. No offshore accounts. No
      personal accounts for a corporate payee.**

## 6. References
- [ ] Two prior clients contacted (name, contact, date, what was said)
- [ ] Any prior work with international donors / UN / U.S. government

## 7. Documents on file
- [ ] Registration / incorporation certificate
- [ ] Tax registration
- [ ] Professional licenses required for the scope
- [ ] Insurance certificates
- [ ] Signed NDA
- [ ] **Signed FCPA rep letter** (`fcpa_rep_letter.md`)

## 8. Red flags — any hit requires a written justification to proceed
- [ ] Refuses or delays signing the rep letter
- [ ] Requests cash, an offshore account, or a third-party payee
- [ ] Compensation tied to award rather than to work performed
- [ ] Markets "relationships" with officials or "ability to get things
      done" rather than technical capability
- [ ] No verifiable address, registry entry, or prior clients
- [ ] Resists audit rights or the anti-bribery clause
- [ ] Adverse media on corruption, fraud, or sanctions
- [ ] Country scores poorly on Transparency International's CPI
      (https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi) — not disqualifying, but it
      raises the bar on everything above
