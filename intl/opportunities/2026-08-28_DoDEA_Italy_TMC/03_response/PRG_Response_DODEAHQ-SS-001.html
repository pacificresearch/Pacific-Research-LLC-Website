2. Capability and Delivery Model

PRG is a service-disabled-veteran-owned prime contractor whose model is program management of qualified execution partners. For this requirement PRG would perform as prime with a small U.S. management cell (program manager, quality manager, WMS administrator) and deliver trade execution through established, licensed host-nation facility-maintenance and specialty firms in Italy under PRG's scheduling, quality control, safety, and reporting systems. In the interest of accurate market research, PRG states its position plainly: PRG is recently established and does not yet hold entity-level past performance managing a facility-maintenance program of this size; its offer to the Government is a compliant SDVOSB prime structure over experienced host-nation execution capacity, with the relevant experience elements below.

  • DoD-standard equipment maintenance discipline: PRG's Managing Director is an AAMI Certified Biomedical Equipment Technician (USAF-trained) who managed over $2M in medical equipment infrastructure at greater than 98% preventive-maintenance and compliance rates under Joint Commission and DoD accreditation standards; directly transferable to PM scheduling, work-order documentation, AQL compliance, and equipment lifecycle reporting under a WMS.
  • Military installation operations: U.S. military veteran; large-scale interagency operations experience (Operation Allies Welcome, 25,000+ evacuees) including installation access, security procedures, and federal compliance reporting.
  • Educational and institutional facilities: delivered through host-nation partners with school and institutional maintenance portfolios; PRG enforces child-safety, escort, and background-screening requirements as prime.
  • Trades coverage: HVACR, electrical, plumbing, fire protection, physical security (CCTV/access control), and commercial kitchen equipment delivered through licensed Italian firms holding required host-nation certifications; OEM-authorized service arrangements for proprietary systems.
  • Recruitment and retention: PRG operates an active recruiting engine (public careers platform plus host-nation channels); site leadership hired locally with retention incentives tied to contract option years.
  • Host-nation compliance: PRG's workflow requires anti-corruption vetting of every partner and hire, host-nation labor-law compliance review, and licensing verification before subcontract award.

3. Industry Feedback on the Draft PWS

  • Lot structure: consider regional lots (e.g., Northern Italy: Aviano/Vicenza/Livorno; Southern Italy: Naples/Sigonella) or campus groupings. This materially widens small business and SDVOSB participation without degrading accountability, and mirrors how host-nation subcontract capacity is actually organized.
  • Non-routine threshold: the €5,000 labor-parts-materials threshold is workable but dated; €7,500 better reflects current EU material and labor costs and will reduce administrative work-order splits.
  • Emergency response times (1 hour duty / 2 hours non-duty): achievable only with on-installation or near-installation staffing. Recommend the PWS state explicitly whether resident site presence is required per campus, as this is the single largest cost driver.
  • Historical WMS data: releasing 24 months of work-order counts by category and campus with the RFP would materially tighten pricing and reduce risk premiums across all offerors.
  • Pricing structure: FFP for scheduled PM per campus; separately priced non-routine pool with NTE thresholds; labor-rate schedule for PWRs and emergencies. Include an economic price adjustment mechanism for host-nation wage determinations and EUR/USD movement on multi-year performance.
  • AQLs: proposed standards are generally consistent with commercial practice if surveillance is sampling-based; 100% inspection regimes on high-volume PM tasks inflate price without quality gain.
  • Mobilization: 60–90 day mobilization with incumbent overlap on life-safety systems (fire protection) protects continuity.
