2. Capability and Delivery Model

PRG is a service-disabled-veteran-owned prime contractor whose model is program management of qualified execution partners. For this requirement PRG would perform as prime with a small U.S. management cell (program manager, quality manager, WMS administrator) and deliver trade execution through established, licensed host-nation facility-maintenance and specialty firms in Japan under PRG's scheduling, quality control, safety, and reporting systems. In the interest of accurate market research, PRG states its position plainly: PRG is recently established and does not yet hold entity-level past performance managing a facility-maintenance program of this size; its offer to the Government is a compliant SDVOSB prime structure over experienced host-nation execution capacity, with the relevant experience elements below.

  • DoD-standard equipment maintenance discipline: PRG's Managing Director is an AAMI Certified Biomedical Equipment Technician (USAF-trained) who managed over $2M in medical equipment infrastructure at greater than 98% preventive-maintenance and compliance rates under Joint Commission and DoD accreditation standards; directly transferable to PM scheduling, work-order documentation, AQL compliance, and equipment lifecycle reporting under a WMS.
  • Military installation operations: U.S. military veteran; large-scale interagency operations experience (Operation Allies Welcome, 25,000+ evacuees) including installation access, security procedures, and federal compliance reporting.
  • Educational and institutional facilities: delivered through host-nation partners with school and institutional maintenance portfolios; PRG enforces child-safety, escort, and background-screening requirements as prime.
  • Trades coverage: facility maintenance, HVACR, sports field and playground inspection/maintenance (CPSI-aligned), and commercial kitchen equipment delivered through licensed Japanese firms; bilingual site management for base access and school coordination.
  • Recruitment and retention: PRG operates an active recruiting engine (public careers platform plus host-nation channels); site leadership hired locally with retention incentives tied to contract option years.
  • Host-nation compliance: PRG's workflow requires anti-corruption vetting of every partner and hire, host-nation labor-law compliance review, and licensing verification before subcontract award.

3. Industry Feedback on the Draft PWS

  • Lot structure: consider two regional lots (Tohoku/Misawa; Kanto Plain: Yokota/Zama/Sagamihara). The regions are ~700 km apart; separate lots reduce mobilization cost, widen small business participation, and match Japanese subcontractor geography.
  • Minor repair threshold: $2,500 is low for current Japanese labor and material costs; $5,000 would reduce administrative order volume and better reflect commercial practice.
  • Response times: state explicitly whether resident on-campus staffing is required per school or whether regional response teams satisfy requirements; this is the dominant cost driver across 968K SF and 8 campuses.
  • Historical data: releasing 24 months of work-order history by school and category with the RFP would tighten all offerors' pricing.
  • Pricing structure: FFP per school for scheduled maintenance; labor-rate schedule for non-routine repairs; economic price adjustment for JPY/USD movement and Japanese wage growth over option years.
  • Playgrounds/fields: require CPSI-certified inspection standards explicitly; monthly visual + annual certified inspection is standard commercial practice.
  • SOFA considerations: clarify expectations for contractor personnel status, base access sponsorship, and vehicle access for subcontractor crews at each installation.
