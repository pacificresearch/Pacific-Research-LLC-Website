# PRG INTERNATIONAL — screening gate (intl-v1)

Run this gate FIRST on any overseas notice, tender, or consultant call,
before any analysis. Lead with one decision:

**BID AS PRIME / PRIME WITH LOCAL PARTNER / PURSUE AS SUBCONTRACTOR /
RESPOND TO SOURCES SOUGHT / REGISTER–PREPOSITION / PASS.**

For a PASS: name the kill criterion, one line each, under 100 words.

The domestic capture-v3 gate in `CLAUDE.md` still governs everything it
covers. This file states what is DIFFERENT overseas — the added kills,
the corrected set-aside law, and the risk/compliance blocks that are
mandatory rather than optional.

---

## A. The set-aside question — get this right, it is not what it looks like

Two different regimes. Never apply one to the other.

### A1. U.S. government buys with an overseas place of performance
FAR 19.000(b) (as amended by the 2022 final rule on applicability of
small business regulations outside the United States):

> (b)(1) Unless otherwise specified in this part — (i) Contracting
> officers **shall** apply this part in the United States and its
> outlying areas; and (ii) Contracting officers **may** apply this part
> outside the United States and its outlying areas.

So SDVOSB/VOSB set-asides overseas are **DISCRETIONARY, not
unavailable.** Overseas awards count toward the agency's small-business
goals, which gives the CO a reason to say yes. This is a live lever:

- On a sources-sought or RFI for overseas work, PRG's response **asks
  the CO to consider a 19.000(b)(1)(ii) set-aside** and states that an
  SDVOSB award at that post counts toward the agency's goals. That is
  advocacy, and it is accurate. Cite the paragraph.
- On an issued solicitation with no set-aside, treat it as full and
  open. Do not claim a preference that was not taken.
- Never write "SDVOSB set-aside required" or imply entitlement abroad.

### A2. UN system, multilateral, and development-bank procurement
No U.S. set-aside regime applies at all. SDVOSB/VOSB is worth **one
line** in a vendor profile or company background as credibility (veteran-
owned, U.S.-registered, CAGE/UEI on file) and **nothing more**. It buys
no preference, no evaluation points, no protest right. Any document that
leans on it in this lane is wrong and gets rewritten.

## B. Hard kills (any one = PASS)

**Inherited from the domestic gate:**
1. Pure resale with no value-add.
2. Bonded or large construction (no bonding capacity).
3. Firm-level licensure PRG does not hold and cannot satisfy through
   key personnel.
4. Set-asides PRG is ineligible for (8(a), HUBZone, WOSB/EDWOSB).
5. Assets that must be held **at proposal time** — FCL, GSA schedule,
   incumbent IDIQ, prior Phase I.
6. Ostensible-subcontractor structures — one indivisible specialty a
   single partner performs end-to-end with no PRG management value.
7. Dead timeline, or stated value > $10M this early.

**Added for international:**

8. **OFAC / sanctions — LEGAL KILL, NON-NEGOTIABLE.** Comprehensively
   sanctioned jurisdictions, any SDN- or SSI-listed counterparty, any
   party on the SAM.gov exclusions list, and any structure that would
   route value to a blocked person. This kill has no override, no
   founder exception, and no "the client says it's fine." Screening
   procedure and the current jurisdiction list live in
   `intl/COMPLIANCE_RAILS.md` §1 — re-check the list every pursuit;
   it changes.
9. **In-country legal entity or in-country bank account required AT
   PROPOSAL TIME.** PRG cannot stand up a registered subsidiary or a
   local account on a bid clock. → **REGISTER–PREPOSITION**, and note
   in the screening doc where a local partner or an agent-of-record
   arrangement would cure it for the next cycle. If the requirement is
   only at *award* and is curable in the award-to-start window, it is
   not a kill — price the setup cost and say so.
10. **Local-national-only / host-country-firm-only.** National
    preference schemes, "registered with the host government's supplier
    registry," domestic-firm-only tenders under borrower procurement
    rules. Not winnable as a U.S. entity. Note whether a local partner
    prime with PRG as sub is realistic; otherwise PASS.
11. **UNGM Level 2 wall — $500K ceiling until PRG turns three.** UNGM
    Level 2 registration (needed for UN business above US $500,000)
    requires the company to have been **established a minimum of 3
    years**, plus three reference letters and financial statements. PRG
    cannot meet that yet. Any UN-system tender above US $500K is
    therefore **PASS (ineligible to register at the required level)**,
    not a judgment call. PRG's UN lane is Basic (≤ $40K) and Level 1
    ($40K–$500K) until the three-year mark. Track the eligibility date
    in `intl/PIPELINE.md`.
12. **No path to lawful performance** — work requiring a visa/work
    authorization category neither PRG nor a local hire can obtain in
    the time available, or requiring an export license PRG will not get.

## C. NOT kills

- **Conflict zones and high-threat posts.** Andrew's standing decision
  (8/18): conflict zones are fine. He is a veteran with TCCC and
  austere-operations background and accepts the risk. Instead of a
  kill, every conflict/high-threat pursuit carries a **mandatory RISK
  block** (§D) and a **higher margin band**. Danger pricing is standard
  in this market — never bid it thin.
- **Overseas place of performance on a U.S. buy.** That is the lane.
  The domestic matcher scores these DOWN; `--intl` mode does not.
- **Founder cannot personally perform.** Same aggregation logic as
  domestic — the model is source-and-manage local experts.
- **PRG has no presence in-country.** Local partners supply presence
  and are **named as partners**, never described as PRG staff or PRG
  offices. See the honesty rules in `intl/WORKFLOW.md`.
- **Foreign-language requirement**, unless it must be held by PRG at
  proposal and no local hire can satisfy it in time.
- **Payment in local currency**, unless combined with a
  non-convertible currency or capital controls that trap the receivable
  — then it is a working-capital question, priced, not a kill.

## D. Mandatory RISK block — every conflict / high-threat pursuit

No pursuit in a high-threat post proceeds without all six lines
answered in `00_screening.md`. An unanswered line is a hold, not a
guess.

1. **Threat picture** — current State Dept Travel Advisory level and
   date, OSAC reporting for the post, and what specifically drives it.
2. **Security and life support, priced** — movement security, PPE, comms
   (sat), secure lodging or camp fees, and whether the contract or the
   post provides any of it. If PRG buys it, it is a direct cost line.
3. **DBA insurance rated for the threat level.** Defense Base Act
   coverage is required on U.S. government service contracts performed
   overseas and is priced as a rate per $100 of payroll — **war-risk
   posts carry a materially higher rate.** Get a rated quote before
   pricing; do not use a CONUS placeholder.
4. **Medevac and casualty coverage** — named provider, activation
   procedure, and who pays. Covers PRG personnel **and local hires.**
5. **Duty of care for local hires** — this is the line that gets skipped
   and it is the one with the most exposure. Written plan: hours,
   movement rules, insurance, next-of-kin contact, and what PRG does if
   a local hire is injured or detained.
6. **Abort criteria** — the stated condition under which PRG stops work
   and withdraws, and what that does to the contract and the invoice.

## E. Mandatory compliance rows — every pursuit, no exceptions

These are compliance-matrix ROWS, not afterthoughts. Detail and
checklists in `intl/COMPLIANCE_RAILS.md`.

| # | Rail | Fires on |
|---|------|----------|
| 1 | OFAC / sanctions / exclusions screen | Every pursuit, every counterparty |
| 2 | **FCPA + local anti-bribery vetting of every agent, partner, and hire** | Every pursuit — the #1 legal risk of this model |
| 3 | Defense Base Act insurance | U.S. government service contracts performed overseas |
| 4 | Export controls / ITAR / EAR screen | Anything technical, any hardware, any technical data crossing a border |
| 5 | Local labor law + contractor-vs-employee classification | Every local hire, per country |
| 6 | Currency, payment terms, FX exposure | Every pursuit |
| 7 | Visa / work authorization / entry requirements | Founder travel and every deployed person |
| 8 | DSSR per-diem and travel rates | Every pursuit with founder travel (i.e. all of them) |
| 9 | Tax presence / permanent-establishment risk | Any pursuit with sustained in-country presence |

## F. Scoring priorities for survivors

Same shape as domestic, re-weighted:

- **Margin band by fulfillment model, country-risk adjusted.** Local-
  expert professional services 35–55%; local-firm subcontract management
  12–25%; founder-delivered advisory 45–65%; value-added supply overseas
  10–20%. **High-threat posts: floor +10 points on the band, never
  below.**
- **Travel economics** — can the founder reach the post, and does one
  trip serve more than one pursuit? Cluster by region.
- **Payment reality** — UN and multilateral net-30 to net-60 with slow
  practice; embassy local-procurement can be fast. Working-capital float
  = local payroll + partner payments carried before first receipt.
- **Registration ceiling** — does PRG hold the registration this buy
  requires TODAY (SAM, UNGM level, bank/borrower portal)? If not, it is
  REGISTER–PREPOSITION, not a bid.
- **Beachhead value** — first overseas past performance is worth more
  than its margin. A clean $60K embassy services award opens the lane.
- **Language and network fit** — Brazil, Peru, China field experience
  and Portuguese/Spanish/Mandarin capability are real discriminators
  where they apply, and are worth nothing where they do not. Do not
  stretch them.
