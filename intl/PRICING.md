# PRG International — pricing model

**Delegated authority (Andrew's standing order 8/17, extended to this
lane):** Claude prices independently from data and presents the finished
number with the package. Never block a bid waiting on pricing input.
Never price below documented direct cost without an explicit strategic
note.

Every international price is built bottom-up in this order. Skipping a
line is how an overseas contract loses money on a bid that looked
profitable.

---

## The build-up

### 1. Local delivery labor
Source in this priority order, and **cite the source and date** in
`04_pricing.md`:
1. A real quote from a vetted local firm or expert (best — it is the
   actual price).
2. Prior PRG rates from `intl/experts/ROSTER.md` for that country and
   discipline, adjusted for elapsed time.
3. Published local-market data: ILOSTAT (https://ilostat.ilo.org/),
   the country's national statistics office, Numbeo for cost-of-living
   cross-checks, and UN/NGO salary scales for comparable roles at post.
4. **UN local salary scales** (https://icsc.un.org/) where a UN agency
   is the buyer — the buyer's own scale is the most defensible anchor
   available and evaluators recognize it.

Then add the **employer burden for the chosen structure** (Rail 5):
- Local firm subcontract → the quote already includes it.
- **EOR → +10–20% on salary**, plus the EOR's own fee.
- Direct contractor → nominally none, but carries reclassification
  risk; if used, note the risk explicitly.
Plus mandatory local additions: social contributions, 13th-month or
statutory bonuses, statutory leave, severance accrual.

### 2. Founder travel — DSSR, priced as a direct cost, never omitted
Per `intl/COMPLIANCE_RAILS.md` Rail 8. Pull the **post-specific rate on
the pricing date** and cite the effective date — DSSR rates change
monthly.
- Lodging + M&IE per diem × days at post, from
  https://aoprals.state.gov/content.asp?content_id=184&menu_id=78
- Airfare from real fares on real dates + a change allowance
- Visa fees, vaccinations, ground transport, airport transfers
- Where applicable, post hardship differential and danger pay — the
  government's own quantification of the post's difficulty, and useful
  evidence when justifying the price
- **Trip count** — be honest: sourcing/vetting trip, mobilization trip,
  and periodic oversight. Two to three trips on a first-year contract is
  normal. Under-counting trips is the most common way this model's
  price goes wrong.

### 3. Security and life support (high-threat posts)
Movement security, PPE, satellite comms, secure lodging or camp fees,
security-driven transport. Whatever the contract or the post does not
provide, PRG buys, and it is a direct line item. Never absorbed.

### 4. Insurance
- **DBA, rated for the actual country** (Rail 3) — rate per $100 of
  payroll; war-risk posts are a multiple of benign posts. **Get a rated
  quote.** A CONUS placeholder is not a number.
- Confirm local-national coverage is included.
- Travel medical and medevac for PRG personnel and local hires.
- General liability / professional liability extended to overseas
  operations — confirm the existing policy's territory clause actually
  covers the country. Most domestic policies do not.

### 5. Compliance and setup costs
The rails are not free. Price them:
- Sanctions and FCPA vetting time (real hours)
- Legal review of local partner agreements
- Local tax or labor advice where Rail 9 fires
- Translation and document legalization / apostille
- Bank wire and correspondent fees, both directions

### 6. Overhead and G&A
PRG's standard rate, plus any pursuit-specific allocation.

### 7. Withholding tax — subtract it, do not discover it
If the buyer withholds local tax on payments to a foreign contractor
(Rail 9 — commonly 5–20%), **the bid price must be grossed up** or the
margin is simply gone. Determine this before pricing, not after the
first invoice.

### 8. Margin — country-risk adjusted

| Fulfillment model | Base band |
|---|---|
| Founder-delivered advisory / consultancy | 45–65% |
| Local-expert professional services (PRG manages) | 35–55% |
| Local-firm subcontract management | 12–25% |
| Value-added supply overseas | 10–20% |

**Adjustments, applied on top:**
- **High-threat / conflict post: floor +10 points, never below.** Danger
  pricing is standard in this market. Bidding a war-risk post at a
  benign-post margin is not competitive, it is a mistake evaluators will
  not reward.
- Currency risk (local-currency contract, volatile or controlled): +5.
- First-in-country pursuit (no local network yet, sourcing cost is real
  and partly unrecoverable): +5.
- Established local partner and a repeat post: −5 is acceptable to win
  recurring work.

## The three checks before any number leaves the folder

### Check 1 — Limitation on subcontracting
Applies when the buy is a U.S. small-business set-aside, **including an
overseas set-aside taken under FAR 19.000(b)(1)(ii)** — the limitation
follows the set-aside, not the geography. 50% of the cost of services
must go to PRG or similarly situated entities; **a local foreign firm
is NOT similarly situated**, so a set-aside award performed entirely by
a local subcontractor fails the test. Structure around it: PRG's own
management, QC, and any W2 or 1099 personnel count toward PRG's half.
On full-and-open and on UN/bank work, the limitation does not apply.

### Check 2 — Working capital float
`float = local payroll + partner payments + travel + insurance premiums,
carried from mobilization to first receipt.`

Use the **practical** payment lag, not the stated terms: UN and
multilateral nominal net-30/60 runs longer in practice; embassy local
procurement can be fast. **Overseas float is worse than domestic**
because travel and insurance are paid up front, before any work is
invoiced. If the float exceeds PRG's capacity, the answer is a smaller
first contract or negotiated advance/milestone payments — not optimism.

### Check 3 — FX stress test
On any non-USD contract, state what a **10% adverse move** does to the
margin. If it wipes the margin out, the band is wrong or the contract
needs a currency clause. Write the number down; do not leave it as a
feeling.
